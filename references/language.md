# Humanizer language rules

Load the relevant language section when the task needs language-specific cleanup.

## English

Use native US English unless the target requires another variant.

- Prefer simple `is`, `are`, `has`, and direct verbs over `serves as`, `stands as`, `showcases`, or `underscores` when the simpler construction is accurate.
- Cut translationese, filler transitions, participial tails, and redundant hedging. Keep qualifiers that carry probability, frequency, approximation, attribution, or permission.
- Preserve material business usage, currency, date conventions, and US/UK variant from the source or target; never convert an ambiguous date or currency by guessing.
- Keep `may`, `must`, `should`, `will`, `can`, `subject to`, `unless`, `up to`, and `at least` distinct where they carry meaning. Preserve tense and aspect: `we're refunding` does not establish `we've refunded`.
- Use contractions where natural for the genre; keep formal or fixed wording where precision requires it. Passive voice can preserve an unknown actor: do not turn `will be sent` into `I'll send` without source support.
- Review repetitive dash-driven emphasis; keep grammatical dashes and en-dash ranges that fit the author, genre, or house style. Do not impose a zero-dash rule or replace every dash with a semicolon.
- Do not over-hyphenate compound phrases in predicate position.
- Avoid false ranges such as `from X to Y` when X and Y do not form a meaningful scale.

Keep natural contractions, necessary repeated nouns, genuine uncertainty, and dictionary-fixed compound spelling. A short list of three, a transition, or formal vocabulary is not automatically a style defect.

## Russian

Write native Russian rather than translated English syntax.

- Preserve modality and aspect: `можем`, `планируем`, `обязаны`, `следует`, `вернём`, `возвращаем`, and `вернули` express different states. Do not erase `обычно`, `примерно`, `не менее`, `до`, `если`, `кроме`, or `при условии`, or change who acts, for smoother prose.
- Preserve exact deadlines and boundary wording. Do not resolve ambiguous `до пятницы` into an invented hour or inclusive/exclusive cutoff. Keep source timezone, currency, amount, and subscription-cancellation conditions.
- Keep the source's `ты/вы/Вы`, singular/plural speaker, and level of formality unless the target explicitly requires a change. Do not add `приносим извинения` or remove an existing apology merely to adjust tone.
- Keep ordinary Russian connectors when they carry a real relation. Cut repeated staging such as `давайте разберёмся`, `здесь важно понять`, and `в этом и заключается главное` only when it adds no meaning. Distinguish `понимаем Ваше недовольство` from an apology for the disputed action.

- Cut канцелярит and empty nominalizations.
- Reduce stock verbs such as `является`, `позволяет`, `обеспечивает`, `способствует`, `направлен на` when a direct verb works.
- Avoid chains of participles, деепричастия, and bureaucratic connectors such as `в рамках`, `в целях`, `в части` unless the genre requires them.
- Avoid literal English contrast structures and unnecessary subject pronouns.
- Use Russian quotation marks and punctuation appropriate to the target publication unless a supplied sample or house style dictates otherwise.
- Keep accepted English product names, code, paths, commands, identifiers, and technical terms unchanged.
- Prefer ordinary business language over translated consulting shorthand. Concrete questions such as `кто принимает решение`, `где лежит информация`, `что приходится проверять вручную`, and `что изменится после работы` are usually clearer in buyer-facing prose.
- Prefer the main action over a setup phrase that only stages it. If the next clause already says what the actor does, remove an opening such as `работа начинается с важного участка` unless that scope is material to the claim.
- Do not remove syntactically correct Russian dashes merely because English prose often overuses them. Remove repetitive `X — это Y` and dash-heavy rhetorical rhythm only when formulaic.
- If a sentence sounds plausible only on a slide or consulting deck, rewrite it as something one business owner could naturally say to another.
- Check Russian lexical compatibility, not only grammar. Avoid literal English-style metaphors where an abstract noun inherits a physical or animate verb that sounds unnatural in Russian. Prefer the concrete operating relation: `задача затрагивает несколько подразделений` rather than work that “fits inside” one function; `контекст не приходится собирать заново` rather than context that “survives” a task or chat.
- In polished editorial and business prose, do not use sentence- or paragraph-initial `Но` as the default transition. Choose the relation precisely: `однако`, `при этом`, `в то же время`, `вместе с тем`, or restructure the sentence. Keep `но` where it is the most natural simple contrast, especially inside a sentence.
- Keep paragraph logic semantic rather than rhythmic. A sentence that explains, qualifies, or closes the preceding paragraph normally stays in that paragraph. Do not isolate one-sentence bridge or “punchline” paragraphs unless the separation has a real editorial function.
- After rewriting, read the Russian sentence as native editorial prose rather than as a translated idea. If the construction is technically grammatical but a Russian editor would normally express the relation more directly, rewrite the relation instead of polishing individual words.
